## What it decided

1. **Same two-half shape as `backend-stack`, `guardrails-toolchain` and `primary-keys`** — portable criterion plus a dated worked case, same premise, same absence of per-claim markers. Shape followed rather than re-derived.
2. **Subject = the identifier a person reads out loud.** `primary-keys` owns the row key and had already published part of this material. **The harvest's first claim about how much was wrong, and the audit that caught it is worth recording.** The authoring pass said *four sentences, all pointed at rather than restated*; opening the file on 2026-08-02 showed it publishes **six things, and two of them are full directives this skill also carries** — *numbers are immutable, never reused, never reassigned, stored exactly as issued*, and *parsing meaning out of a number is banned*. That is duplicated directive text, which the write-once rule governs. **Resolved as deliberate duplication with a named owner and an index**, not by deleting either copy: a repo can install one skill and not the other, both bans bear on the key decision as directly as on the numbering one, and the index paragraph now sits beside them in `primary-keys` with this skill named as owner. **The claim that everything was pointed at rather than restated was a count of another document's contents, made without opening it** — the defect class this repo has recorded most often, in a sentence describing how carefully the harvest avoided it.
3. **Central claim: a counter row incremented inside the caller's transaction is what every other property falls out of** — gaplessness, atomicity with the business write, survivability across a data move. Marked *uncertain*: the fifth skill in this set whose central claim is, on no measurement.
4. **The record's honesty is the part most worth carrying, and it is about a negative result.** The pass searched one jurisdiction's accounting statute and its banking regulator's instruction for a gapless-numbering requirement and **found none**, and it recorded that rather than burying it — then made journals gapless anyway, on the stated ground of *cheap insurance against a standing audit expectation* plus two real bonuses. **The conversion carries the search as a search**, and the do-not-cite list says plainly it is not a finding that no such law exists.
5. **The rejected alternatives are named with the mechanism each lost on**, not the category: engine sequences for gap-tolerant classes (lost on replication, and on one mechanism beating two), gapless everywhere (serializes everything for no evidence of need), gapless nowhere (rejected narrowly, for journals only), a pattern-string format language (lost on a **recorded cross-domain counter-contamination defect** in the predecessor system), a two-digit checksum default, and random business identifiers (the row key already provides opacity).
6. **The check-digit directive names the corpus favourite and why it lost** — **Luhn** misses the 09↔90 transposition — which is `enforceable-rules`' *Distrust what the agent picks* with a concrete instance.
7. **Three layer clauses were written into the new skill at authoring rather than found later**, all conversion-dated 2026-08-02: the number-parsing ban reaches query text and a reporting tool's expression language; the sequence grep must point at mapping annotations and non-SQL changelogs; the wall-clock ban reaches `now()` in query text and column defaults. **All three are findings this session's layer checks produced on sibling skills hours earlier**, applied forward instead of being rediscovered.

## What the checks found on the way in

**The evidence-order check ran and passed**: every `###` in `evidence.md` names a real section of the directive text and they run in the directive text's order, verified by script rather than by reading. **The dangling-pointer check ran and passed**: both files link only within the skill directory, no `P-n` or `B-n` ids appear, and no sentence references the external record by its identifier — a consumer cannot resolve `ADR-0024` and would read it as their own repository's numbering.

**All five of `enforceable-rules`' incompleteness checks were then run against it, the same day it was published** — the first time any skill here has had them at authoring rather than after. What they found:

- **Token-placement: three de-named algorithms, in text written an hour earlier.** The check-digit directive said *a three-table scheme* where the source names **Verhoeff**, and `evidence.md` said *the corpus-dominant algorithm* and *a two-digit scheme* where the source names **Luhn** and **mod-97**. **The skill's own subject is naming the loser** — it publishes *Distrust what the agent picks* as its ground for the algorithm-in-stored-config rule — and the draft described three of the four algorithms it was declining. **That is twice on the same day that this check found its worst instances in prose written hours earlier rather than in inherited text** — the other being the three descriptions in that morning's layer clauses. The pattern now has two occasions behind it, not a rate.
- **Predicate: two directives governed acts the trigger did not name.** *Validate at every ingress* is about **adding an entry point that accepts a number**, which is the act whose failure mode the directive itself states — validation living in one of three entry points. And the parsing ban extends to reports, so **writing a report that filters or groups on a number** fired nothing. Both added. **One it could not close**: the birth-fact argument bites hardest when a branch closes or an organisation restructures, and *reorganising* is not an act anyone loads a numbering skill before.
- **Composite-shape: the sharpest entry is the relief ladder's own step.** Demoting counter granularity to per-branch series **changes what gaplessness means mid-life** — numbers before are gapless per tenant, numbers after are gapless per branch, and the production invariant quietly starts asserting something weaker. Resolved as a condition: a granularity change is a new series key and therefore a new format version at a rollover, never a configuration flip inside a live period. **And a cross-skill collision nobody had stated**, which then took two corrections to get right. First draft: *three disciplines competing for the tail of one transaction* — this skill's counter, `async-handoff`'s outbox append, and a balance update. **Wrong on the mechanism**: an outbox append inserts a new row and takes no contended lock, so it does not compete at all; what competes is the counter row against `money-storage` `M-39`'s optional mutable balance row, and the failure is a **deadlock when two transactions take them in different orders**. Second draft answered that with *decide one lock order and write it down* — **which is `ai-maintainer-principles`' own named example of a requirement that cannot be documented**, published in this same skill set. Found by grepping for `lock order` before publishing rather than after. Final form takes the two answers that directive allows: remove the second contended row, or confine every such transaction to one named operation. **Stated from four sides** — here, `money-storage`, `async-handoff` (which says it does *not* compete, because a reader of all three needs that), and `ai-maintainer-principles`, which records it as the worked instance where its abstract form failed to stop a defect and its named example caught one.
- **Layer: the class list exists twice.** The enumeration is code and the formats that give each class its behaviour are seeded data, so the compile-checked half makes the *name* unmisspellable and says nothing about whether the class is configured. A class in one and not the other fails at first issuance, inside a business transaction, or is dead configuration nothing reports.
- **Enumeration: clean.** The directive count in this file was verified by script rather than asserted, and the one count in the skill — seven classes — sits adjacent to the table that lists them.
